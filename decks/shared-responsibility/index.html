The provider keeps the cloud safe. Keeping what we put in it safe is our job.

Pause on the question and let people nod. Most people believe the provider handles security.

Reveal "Partly". Then the stat: Gartner's well-known prediction (2019) that through 2025, 99% of cloud security failures would be the customer's fault. That's not because the cloud is unsafe. It's because the parts that go wrong are usually ours: settings, passwords, access.

Let's start with something everyone understands

Pizza as a Service 🍕

You do it Someone else does it
🏠Homemade≈ On-premises

🍽️Dine out≈ SaaS

Even at the best restaurant, who you invite and what you say is still down to you.

Each column hands more of the work to someone else. Homemade: you do everything. Take & bake: someone makes the pizza, you cook it. Delivered: it arrives ready, and you supply the table. Dine out: they do it all.

The punchline: the top layer never moves. In cloud terms, that's our data and who we let in.

In every one of these, if you hand your key to a stranger, that's on you. In the cloud, your keys are passwords, accounts and access rights.

A second analogy that lands well with boards: housing. The further right, the fewer chores. But the hotel can't stop you from giving your key card away.

That's the theme of today: identity, meaning who has a key, always stays with us.

Three things that are always ours

🗂️

Our data

What we store, who can see it, how long we keep it, and whether it's backed up.

🪪

Our identities

Who has an account, what they can do, MFA, and switching off leavers quickly.

💻

Our devices

The laptops and phones people use to get in. A compromised laptop gets into a perfectly secure cloud.

Whether it's IaaS, PaaS or SaaS, these three rows never change colour.

Whether it's a server we rent or an app we subscribe to, these three stay with us. That's true for AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, Microsoft 365, Salesforce and every other provider.

The principle

You can outsource the work.
You can't outsource the accountability.

THE DAILY BYTE
Morning edition · 50p
ACME CORP LEAKS 2 MILLION CUSTOMER RECORDS
Cloud provider “not at fault”, says regulator
👥

Customers don't read cloud contracts. They read headlines, and the headline has our name in it.

⚖️

Regulators hold the organisation that owns the data accountable. Under UK GDPR, that's us as data controller.

🧾

Contracts limit the provider's liability. They rarely cover our losses.

Make it real. The headline never names the cloud provider. Under UK GDPR we're the controller, and the provider is a processor. We remain accountable for choosing them and for configuring things properly.

"Acme Corp" and the newspaper are illustrative, not a real story.

Take these back to your teams

Six questions worth asking on Monday

  1. For our key systems, do we know which are IaaS, PaaS or SaaS, and who owns each layer?
  2. Is MFA switched on for every account, especially admins?
  3. How fast do we remove access when someone leaves or changes role?
  4. Are we backing up our SaaS data (email, files, CRM) and not just our servers?
  5. If one cloud location went down, what would stop, and are we OK with that?
  6. Who checks our cloud settings for mistakes, and how often?

None of these need technical knowledge to ask. If a team can't answer one confidently, that's useful information.

Tip: turn this list into a simple RACI (responsible, accountable, consulted, informed) for each major system.

Same idea, different words

What you'll hear from the big three

Amazon Web Services

“Security of the cloud vs security in the cloud”

AWS secures the infrastructure. Customers secure what they build and store on it.

Microsoft Azure

“The shared responsibility model”

Microsoft publishes a layer-by-layer chart much like the one we used earlier.

Google Cloud

“Shared fate”

Google aims to help more through secure defaults and guidance. The split of responsibility still applies.

Different branding, same question: which layers are ours?

Useful when people come back from vendor meetings with new phrases. Different branding, same principle.
